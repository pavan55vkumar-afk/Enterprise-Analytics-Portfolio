# Product Conversion Funnel A/B Testing

## Business Context

The product team proposed a new checkout flow to increase conversion rates. However, before deploying the redesign to 100% of website traffic, they required statistical evidence that the conversion lift was meaningful and not due to random sampling noise.

## Business Questions

1. Did the variant experience a higher conversion rate than the control?
2. Is the observed conversion lift statistically significant?
3. What is the estimated range of the true conversion lift (95% confidence interval)?
4. What is the rollout recommendation for leadership?

## Dataset

- 5,000 synthetic experiment participants split between control and variant.
- Fields: user_id, group, device, converted (0/1), and revenue.

*Note: All data is synthetic, generated by the included script for portfolio practice only.*

## My Approach

1. Split the logs into Control and Variant populations.
2. Formulated the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$: conversion rates are equal) and Alternative Hypothesis ($H_a$: variant conversion rate is greater).
3. Computed the proportions, pooled conversion rate, and standard error of the difference from scratch.
4. Calculated a two-proportion Z-statistic and calculated the two-tailed P-value.
5. Established the 95% Confidence Interval (CI) for the absolute difference.
6. Combined statistical evidence with business context for a rollout recommendation.

## KPIs

- Conversion rate (CR)
- Absolute conversion rate lift
- Relative conversion rate lift
- Z-statistic
- P-value
- 95% Confidence Interval (CI)

## Findings

- Control Group (Current Flow): **n = 2,495** | Conversions = **293** | Conversion Rate = **11.74%**
- Variant Group (New Checkout): **n = 2,505** | Conversions = **354** | Conversion Rate = **14.13%**
- Observed Conversion Rate Lift: **+2.39%** absolute (relative lift of **+20.35%**)
- 95% Confidence Interval (CI): **[+0.53% to +4.25%]**
- Calculated Z-Statistic: **2.5157**
- Calculated P-Value: **0.01188** (statistically significant at $\alpha = 0.05$)

## Recommendation

- **Proceed with Rollout**: Because the conversion rate lift is statistically significant ($p = 0.01188 < 0.05$), proceed with rolling out the new checkout flow.
- **Implement a Staged Release**: Expose the new flow to 25% of traffic initially, monitoring errors and page load times by device before going to 100%.
- **Track Secondary Guardrail Metrics**: Ensure that average order value (AOV) and return rates are not negatively affected by the new checkout.

## Outcome

The experiment provided statistically supported evidence in favor of the variant, allowing product leadership to make an evidence-based rollout decision.
